EMF — electromagnetic fields — is the single most confusing topic in infrared sauna shopping. Every brand claims "low EMF." Almost none tells you the number. Here's what's actually going on.

## What EMF is and why saunas produce it

Anything that runs electricity generates an electromagnetic field — your phone, your microwave, and yes, your sauna's heaters. Infrared sauna heaters draw 1,000+ watts, so the fields near the panels are stronger than near most household devices. The health science on low-level EMF exposure is genuinely unsettled, which is why "may help you relax" is as far as honest health claims go — but many buyers reasonably prefer less exposure, and manufacturers compete on it.

## What milligauss (mG) means

EMF strength in this context is measured in milligauss (mG). Two things matter: the number, and the distance it was measured at. Fields weaken dramatically with distance, so "2 mG at 6 inches" and "2 mG at 2 inches" describe very different exposures. Always ask at what distance a figure was taken — a number without a distance is close to meaningless.

## "Low EMF" vs "ultra-low EMF": labels without a referee

Here's the uncomfortable truth we found comparing specs: these designations are manufacturer marketing tiers, not standardized certifications. No independent body certifies "ultra-low EMF" for saunas. Dynamic uses "ultra-low EMF" on the Venice Elite, Elite, and Madrid, and "low EMF" on the Barcelona and Andora — but publishes no milligauss figures for any of them, so you can't verify the difference between the two tiers or compare them to competitors.

## The one brand that publishes a number

JNH Lifestyles quotes an average of 2–3 mG at 6 inches for the [Joyous 2-Person](amazon:jnh-joyous-2-person). That's a concrete, distance-specified figure — and it's the only one in this guide. Whether 2–3 mG satisfies your standards is your call, but a published measurement beats an unquantified badge. This transparency is a core reason the Joyous is our best-value pick. ## Carbon vs. ceramic and EMF

Heater design affects EMF. Carbon panels — large, flat, low-temperature surfaces — generally produce lower EMF than older ceramic rod heaters, which concentrate more power in a smaller element. Every cabin in this guide uses carbon-family panels, so you're already on the favorable side of this particular divide.

## Practical advice

If EMF matters to you: favor the Joyous for its published figure, or Dynamic's ultra-low EMF models ([Venice Elite](/saunas/dynamic-venice-elite-2-person/), Elite, Madrid) for the design intent. Sit centered on the bench rather than pressed against a panel. And keep claims modest — lower EMF is a reasonable preference, not a medical prescription.
