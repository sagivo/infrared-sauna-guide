Short answer: every model in this guide is indoor-only. Longer answer: here's what that means for where yours goes, and what "outdoor infrared sauna" actually requires. If you haven't picked a model yet, start with our [best infrared saunas of 2026](/posts/best-infrared-saunas-2026/) — all ten are indoor cabins.

## Why these are indoor-only

The cabins in this guide — Dynamic, JNH, Radiant, HeatWave, SunRay — are built from interior-grade hemlock or cedar with exposed electronics, Bluetooth modules, and chromotherapy lighting. They're engineered for climate-controlled rooms. Put one on an uncovered patio and rain, humidity cycling, UV, and freeze-thaw will warp the wood, corrode connections, and kill the electronics. Every warranty here assumes indoor installation; outdoor placement voids it.

## What a real outdoor infrared sauna requires

Outdoor-capable infrared saunas exist, but they're a different product: weather-sealed construction, often a covered or roofed design, GFCI-protected electrical, and placement on a proper foundation — not a lawn. They cost more and come from specialty makers, not the Amazon tier this guide covers. If your heart is set on a backyard sauna, budget for that category specifically rather than dragging an indoor cabin outside.

## The covered-patio gray zone

A fully covered, enclosed patio — roof, walls, protection from wind-driven rain — can work for an indoor-rated cabin in mild climates. But "covered" has to mean genuinely weatherproof, and temperature swings still stress the wood and electronics. When in doubt, keep it inside.

## Best indoor spots, ranked

**Basement.** The most popular choice for good reason: space is usually available, a cool ambient room doesn't hurt an infrared cabin (it heats you, not the air), and the sauna's presence doesn't dominate living space. Finished or unfinished both work — just keep it dry.

**Garage.** Excellent ventilation, concrete floors that shrug off sweat, and easy electrical access (garages often already have 20A circuits — handy for the HeatWave Santa Fe). The downside is temperature: an uninsulated garage in winter means longer heat-up times.

**Spare bathroom.** The most moisture-tolerant room in the house, and plumbing-adjacent for post-sauna showers. Space is the constraint — only compact models like the [Dynamic Barcelona](amazon:dynamic-barcelona-1-2-person) or [Dynamic Elite](amazon:dynamic-elite-1-person) fit most bathrooms.

**Bedroom or home office.** Maximum convenience — roll out of bed and into the heat. But the sauna becomes furniture you live with, and the 120V cord needs a nearby outlet. Works best with smaller cabins.

## Placement checklist

- **Outlet:** within cord reach of a grounded outlet — no extension cords for 1,500W sustained loads. See our [electrical guide](/posts/infrared-sauna-electrical-requirements/).
- **Assembly clearance:** you need working room around the cabin during the build — corner units like the Santa Fe need access on two sides.
- **Floor:** something that tolerates sweat — concrete, tile, or a mat under the cabin. Avoid bare carpet.
- **Ceiling height:** most cabins here are 65–75" tall; confirm your ceiling clears the assembled height plus a margin.
- **Ventilation:** infrared cabins don't steam like traditional saunas, but a room with some airflow stays fresher.

Measure twice, check the current price on Amazon once, and put the sauna where you'll actually use it — convenience beats the theoretically perfect room every time.
