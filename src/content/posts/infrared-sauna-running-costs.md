Sticker price gets all the attention, but the true cost of an infrared sauna has four parts. Here's each one, with real numbers.

## 1. Purchase: $699–$2,799

This is the big one — 90%+ of first-year cost. In this guide: the [HigherDOSE blanket](amazon:higherdose-sauna-blanket) at $699, 1-person cabins from ~$1,199 ([Dynamic Elite](amazon:dynamic-elite-1-person)), 2-person cabins ~$1,500–$2,300 ([JNH Joyous](amazon:jnh-joyous-2-person), [Venice Elite](amazon:dynamic-venice-elite-2-person)), and 3-person cabins ~$1,800–$2,800 ([Santa Fe](amazon:heatwave-santa-fe-3-person), [Madrid](amazon:dynamic-madrid-3-person)). Prices swing with sales, so check the current price on Amazon — buying on a dip can save more than any efficiency difference ever will.

## 2. Electrical work: $0 for nine of ten

Every model here except the HeatWave Santa Fe plugs into a standard 120V/15A outlet — zero installation cost. The Santa Fe needs a 120V/20A circuit; if you don't have one where the sauna goes, an electrician's visit is a real line item. Get a local quote, but don't be surprised if it narrows the price gap between the Santa Fe and the plug-and-play [Dynamic Madrid](amazon:dynamic-madrid-3-person). Full details in our [electrical guide](/posts/infrared-sauna-electrical-requirements/).

## 3. Electricity per session: cents, not dollars

The formula: **watts × hours × your $/kWh ÷ 1000**. At ~$0.17/kWh (rough US average), 45-minute sessions:

| Model | Watts | Cost/session |
|---|---|---|
| HigherDOSE blanket | 350–420 | ~$0.05 |
| SunRay Sedona | 1290 | ~$0.16 |
| JNH Joyous | 1540 | ~$0.20 |
| HeatWave Santa Fe | 1805 | ~$0.23 |

Four sessions a week at $0.20: about **$3.50/month, ~$42/year**. Even daily use lands around $5–$8/month. Your rate matters — at $0.30/kWh (California, Hawaii) roughly double these figures; at $0.10/kWh, halve them. Either way, electricity is the smallest ownership cost by far.

## 4. Maintenance: minimal

Infrared cabins are low-maintenance appliances:

- **After each session:** wipe down benches and backrests, leave the door open to air out.
- **Occasionally:** treat hemlock or cedar with a sauna-safe wood conditioner if it looks dry; vacuum heater guards.
- **Every few years:** heaters eventually wear — replacement panels are available from manufacturers, and this is where a 5–7 year warranty earns its keep.
- **Blanket:** wipe the interior surface after use per HigherDOSE's care instructions.

Budget effectively $0–$50/year for consumables. There's no water, no stones, no chimney — none of the upkeep a traditional sauna demands.

## The bottom line

A cabin you buy for ~$1,800 and use four times a week costs roughly **$1,850 in year one and ~$50–$100/year after** — the purchase price is the story, and everything else is a footnote. That makes two things matter most: buying the right size the first time (see our [1-person vs 2-person guide](/posts/1-person-vs-2-person-infrared-sauna/)), and buying on sale. Check the current price on Amazon before you commit.
